Creating a Good Online Presence: What Is the Best Approach?

My thoughts on showcasing game development projects, building professional connections and standing out in the creative industries.

Introduction

As someone studying Game Design and Development, I believe that creating a good online presence is becoming increasingly important for anyone hoping to establish a career within the games industry. Learning to program, understanding game engines and creating enjoyable games are obviously important, but how can a potential employer recognise those skills if they have never seen my work?

Personally, I think an online presence should be more than simply having accounts on different websites. It should provide an accurate picture of who I am, what I can create and how I approach development. I want someone visiting my portfolio to understand my interests and abilities without having to search through dozens of unrelated posts.

However, I also believe there is a balance to consider. Trying to appear active on every available platform could become time-consuming, especially while studying and developing projects. So, what is the best approach to building an online presence that is both professional and manageable?

Establishing My Online Presence

One of the main ways I have started establishing my online presence is through my personal portfolio website. I wanted a central place where I could introduce myself, explain my technical skills and showcase the projects I have completed throughout my studies.

I chose to build my website using HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with the site hosted through GitHub Pages. This means the website itself can also demonstrate some of my web development abilities rather than being just a collection of screenshots.

I believe having my own website offers an advantage because I can control how my work is presented. Rather than relying entirely on the layout of a social media profile, I can organise projects into sections, provide descriptions and make it easier for visitors to find the information that interests them.

At the same time, I don't think having a personal website is enough by itself. It needs to be supported by other platforms where developers, recruiters and potential collaborators can discover my work.

Choosing the Right Platforms

There are many websites where creative professionals can promote themselves, but I believe choosing the right ones is more useful than trying to use all of them. Different platforms serve different purposes, so I would rather focus on those that genuinely support my goals within game development.

LinkedIn

LinkedIn is useful for establishing a professional identity and connecting with people working in the industry. I think it provides an opportunity to communicate my education, skills and experience in a format that employers are familiar with. I would use it to follow studios, learn about roles that interest me and maintain connections with other developers.

GitHub

For programming, I consider GitHub particularly valuable because it allows me to show more than the finished result. A playable game might demonstrate that a feature works, but a well-organised repository can also help someone understand how the code is structured and documented. The GitHub documentation on README files explains how repositories can include useful information about a project, which I think is important when presenting work to others.

itch.io and Game Development Communities

I would also consider using itch.io to publish playable versions of my games. The platform's creator guidance explains how developers can create project pages and share their games. I think allowing people to experience a game themselves can be more convincing than only showing screenshots.

Communities focused on Unity, GameMaker and independent game development could also be useful places to ask questions, share progress and learn from other developers. However, I believe participation should be genuine rather than simply posting links and expecting people to visit them.

Showcasing My Game Development Projects

Personally, I think the strongest part of a game developer's online presence should be the work they have actually created. It is easy to list programming languages or game engines on a profile, but showing a finished project provides evidence of how those skills have been applied.

For example, one of the games I have developed is Galactic Escape, a mobile endless runner created using Unity. The player controls a spaceship, moves between lanes and avoids incoming asteroids. When presenting this project, I think it is useful to explain the gameplay concept, the engine used and the systems involved, rather than simply displaying the game's title.

Other projects, including Undead Survival and Galaxy Defense, were developed using GameMaker Studio. Showing different games allows me to demonstrate that I have experience with more than one engine and that I have worked on different styles of gameplay.

I also believe development screenshots can be valuable. A main menu or gameplay image shows what the game looks like, while an image of a system being developed could help explain the technical work behind it. Where appropriate, I would include a short description of the challenges involved and what I learned from solving them.

However, I don't think every experiment or unfinished prototype needs to appear on the main portfolio. I would rather select projects that demonstrate relevant skills and present them clearly. Smaller experiments could still be useful on GitHub or in development updates, but the main website should highlight the work I most want people to see.

How Can I Stand Out to Potential Employers?

One of the biggest challenges with creating an online presence is standing out when so many other developers are also showcasing their work. I don't believe the solution is to make exaggerated claims or fill a portfolio with unnecessary effects. Instead, I think clarity, honesty and evidence of personal development are much more valuable.

If someone visits my portfolio, I want them to be able to identify the games I have made, the technologies I have used and the areas of development that interest me. I think this is especially important when applying for junior roles, where employers may be interested in potential and willingness to learn as well as previous experience.

I would also try to explain my individual contributions to collaborative projects. If a game was created by a group, I believe it is important to acknowledge the other developers and clearly describe which areas I worked on. Taking credit for an entire team's work would not reflect the professional image I want to establish.

Another way to stand out is to make projects easy to explore. Clear navigation, readable descriptions, screenshots and working links can make a portfolio more useful. I think even an impressive game could be overlooked if visitors cannot quickly understand what it is or how to access it.

Promoting and Maintaining My Work

I don't think an online presence is something that can be completed once and then forgotten about. As I develop new games and improve my skills, I want my portfolio to reflect that progress.

For example, when I finish a new project, I could add a page explaining its gameplay, development tools and key features. I could then share that page through LinkedIn or an appropriate game development community. This would give people a reason to revisit my work rather than seeing exactly the same content every time.

However, I would prefer to post meaningful updates rather than publish content simply to appear active. A short explanation of a mechanic I implemented or a problem I solved would be more useful than an update that provides no real information.

Maintenance is also important from a technical perspective. Broken links, missing images and outdated descriptions can make a website appear neglected. Since my portfolio is hosted through GitHub Pages, I can continue updating the website as my projects change, but I still need to check that everything displays correctly after making changes.

I also think it is sensible to consider what should not be shared publicly. Private information, confidential material and assets that I do not have permission to redistribute should not be uploaded just to make a portfolio look more impressive. Maintaining a professional online presence also means being responsible about what I publish.

My Advice to Other Game Development Students

If I were giving advice to another student who wanted to improve their online presence, my first suggestion would be to start small. A simple portfolio with two or three clearly explained projects can be more useful than an ambitious website that is difficult to navigate or never gets finished.

I would also recommend being honest about your abilities and contributions. Nobody expects a student to know everything, and I believe showing how you learn and overcome challenges can be just as valuable as presenting a polished final result.

Another suggestion would be to use each platform for a clear purpose. A personal website can act as the central portfolio, GitHub can provide technical evidence and LinkedIn can help with professional networking. If someone has playable games, itch.io could give visitors an opportunity to try them directly.

Finally, I would encourage students to keep improving their existing work rather than worrying about competing with every other developer online. I think a portfolio should demonstrate individual progress and interests, not attempt to copy someone else's personality or career path.

Conclusion

Overall, I believe creating a good online presence is an important part of preparing for a career in the creative industries, particularly within game development. Technical skills and creative ideas matter, but having somewhere to demonstrate those abilities makes it easier for other people to recognise them.

For me, the best approach is to maintain a personal portfolio supported by relevant platforms such as GitHub, LinkedIn and potentially itch.io. I want to focus on presenting my strongest projects, explaining my contributions and keeping the information accurate as I continue learning.

I don't believe a successful online presence needs to be complicated or involve posting something every day. Instead, I think it should be clear, professional and genuinely representative of the person behind the work.

Ultimately, my aim is not simply to get more people to visit a website. It is to create a place where my projects, skills and enthusiasm for game development can speak for themselves.
